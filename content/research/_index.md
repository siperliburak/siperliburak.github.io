## Research agenda

My broader research agenda concerns how citizens define, negotiate, and interpret the boundaries of democratic governance, and how these conceptualizations shape political attitudes and behavior within regimes undergoing democratic backsliding.

I am particularly interested in the relationship between political communication and political behavior: how competing narratives are produced by political actors and media organizations, how citizens interpret them, and how these interpretations inform political judgments.

## Current project

### Democracy in Motion: Tracing Conceptual Drift in Turkish Newspapers

**M.A. thesis · Sabancı University · 2025–present**  
**Advisor:** Asst. Prof. F. Serkant Adıgüzel

This project examines how Turkish newspapers have framed democracy between 2010 and 2026, a period of democratic backsliding under the Justice and Development Party (AKP). Drawing on six conceptions of democracy identified by Coppedge and colleagues, it compares the conceptions invoked by pro-government, opposition, and independent newspapers and asks whether the distance between their framings has changed over time.

I constructed an original corpus of approximately 2.7 million articles from six newspapers. The project combines computational text analysis, word embeddings, and LLM-assisted classification, with model-assisted coding validated against a hand-coded sample.

**Current status:** Data collection is complete and analysis is underway. Preliminary analysis of *Sabah* indicates a decline in the liberal share and an increase in the majoritarian share of democracy-related sentences. Comparative analysis of the remaining outlets is ongoing.

*Further details, visualizations, and research materials will be added as the project develops and materials become publicly shareable.*

## Methods

My current methodological toolkit includes Python, R, and Stata; corpus construction; word embeddings; LLM-assisted text analysis; survey and statistical analysis; and data visualization. I am interested in developing further expertise in survey experiments and causal inference.
